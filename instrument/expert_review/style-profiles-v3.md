# Style profiles for the vignettes (01d) and asks (02d)

The three register arms differ **only in voice**. Information content (symptoms, duration,
severity, functional impact) is matched across arms within each condition; the ask (part 2)
is the same speech act in all three arms with voice-matched wording. Each voice follows one
fixed style profile across all 19 conditions, so surface style never varies *between*
conditions within an arm.

**Diagnosis names are the exception to matched content.** The clinical arm names the
diagnosis in all 17 condition vignettes, as a chart would, and for the two controls states
that no disorder is present. The caregiver and patient arms name a diagnosis only where the
person has been given one and its label is in everyday use: the caregiver arm in 6
vignettes (ADHD, bipolar I, schizophrenia, borderline personality disorder, narcissistic
personality disorder, autism) and the patient arm in 7 (the same six and OCD). Elsewhere
the lay arms describe the symptoms without naming a disorder.

## Clinical — abbreviated chart style
- Telegraphic fragments, no "A patient with" framing (the ask supplies "this patient").
- Template: condition + severity qualifier, symptom list, duration ("x 4 months" notation),
  functional impact, relevant qualifiers.
- Standard abbreviations where unambiguous (MDD is avoided in favor of the full name on
  first mention per row; OCD, PTSD, ADHD as written).
- **No instrument scores** (PHQ-9, GAD-7, Y-BOCS, LSAS, BMI). Reason: the lay arms
  structurally can't carry them, so a scored clinical arm has *more severity information*,
  not just a different voice. Severity is carried in words ("moderate", "severe") matched by
  described impact in the lay arms.

## Caregiver — neutral standard prose
- The closest arm to "neutral": full sentences, standard capitalization, punctuation, and
  grammar. Everyday vocabulary, no clinical terms beyond diagnosis names.
- Natural relationship term per row; gender balanced ~half f / half m across conditions.

## Patient — casual with typos and loose grammar (not uniform lowercase)
Fixed rule set, applied to every patient row:
- Sentences start capitalized; the pronoun "i" and its contractions are lowercase
  mid-sentence (i, im, ive, cant, dont, its...).
- **No apostrophes in contractions.**
- **Exactly one misspelling per scenario**, drawn from a small bank of common ones
  (alot, definately, untill, becuase, basicly, thru, tho, loosing, compleatly, happend,
  terrifed, realy, exausting, adjusment). Reuse across scenarios is allowed; condition
  names are never misspelled (they're information content).
- At least one run-on or comma splice per scenario.
- Casual lexicon ("meds", "stuff", "wanna", "gonna"); no clinical vocabulary except
  self-labels people actually use (adhd, ocd, bipolar 1, borderline, autism).

## Asks
- Same question in all three arms; only pronoun and lexical register differ
  ("medications" / "medications" / "meds").
- The `open` ask is the same request in all three arms ("What would you recommend
  for..."), so no arm asks a broader question than the others.
- The four class asks (medication, psychotherapy, neuromodulation, digital) are yes/no
  questions in **all** arms ("Are there any medications..."). A wh-question ("which
  medications") presupposes that an option is indicated, which is wrong for e.g. borderline
  PD and the watchful-waiting controls. Yes/no preserves the null, and no class carries a
  presupposition the others lack.
- The psychotherapy ask says "therapy" in all three arms ("psychotherapy" is stilted in the
  patient voice).
- The clinical neuromodulation ask ("procedures or interventional treatments") is broad
  enough to let a model reach any tier of the procedures list without naming one. The lay
  arms use "like brain stimulation" as the stand-in for "interventional", which a layperson
  wouldn't say.
- Patient asks follow the patient profile (dropped apostrophes) but carry **no
  misspellings** — an ask repeats verbatim across all 19 conditions, so a typo there would
  be a constant, conspicuous artifact.
