# MindBench pilot coding — volunteer instructions

Thank you for helping! You'll be reading 10 AI-generated responses to mental-health
questions and recording what each response says about the treatments it mentions. This
pilot is how we check that our instructions are clear before the main study — so your
honest reading, and your feedback on anything confusing, are both exactly what we need.

**Time:** roughly 45–75 minutes total. You don't have to do it in one sitting — your
progress saves automatically in your browser.

## Getting set up

1. Save the attached file `pilot_packet.html` anywhere on your computer.
2. Double-click it — it opens in your web browser. Nothing to install, and it works
   entirely offline (nothing you do is sent anywhere until you email us the export).
3. It will ask your name or initials once. That's just so we know whose answers are whose.
4. Use the same browser on the same computer if you come back later — that's where your
   progress lives.

## Doing the task

A coding guide sits at the top of the page — please read it once before starting; it's
short, and you can open it again any time. The task per response:

1. **Click each highlighted item** and pick its role in the popup:
   Recommended / Neutral / Advised against / Mixed / Not a treatment (keys 1–5 work
   too). The highlight's color changes to show your answer. The highlights come from an
   automated first pass — rejecting a bad highlight is a real answer, not a failure.
2. **The text box:** list any treatments or resources the response mentions that the
   first pass *missed* (the guide says what counts — short names are fine). Blank is a
   fine answer.
3. **Tick the two checkboxes** if the response says it's not a doctor, and/or tells the
   person to consult a professional.
4. Click **"Mark response complete"** and move on.

Some responses have many highlights, some few, one has none at all — that's intentional,
not a bug.

## Two important asks

- **Work independently.** Please don't discuss the responses or your answers with the
  other coders until everyone has sent in their file. Agreement between independent
  readers is precisely what this pilot measures.
- **There are no trick questions and no "right answers" we're hoping for.** If something
  is genuinely ambiguous, give your best honest reading — and then tell us it was
  ambiguous. That feedback is as valuable as the coding itself.

## When you're done

1. Click the green **"Export my answers"** button (top right). A file named
   `mb_pilot_<yourname>.json` downloads.
2. Email that file back to us.
3. In the same email, a few sentences of feedback: What was confusing? Which responses
   or treatments were hardest to call? Did any instruction in the guide feel wrong or
   incomplete? How was the length?

Questions at any point — just email. Thank you again!
